# Profile Classification Calibration Tests

These scenarios are qualitative regression tests for the prompt or a future classifier. They are not real-user scores or fixed answer templates. Every assessment must separate profile from maturity, distinguish Thinking/Execution/Impact, and explain uncertainty.

## 1. Craft-led independent designer
**Evidence:** Shipped interface case studies show interaction quality, accessibility, prototyping, and consistent execution. No evidence of system governance or sustained organizational impact.
**Expected:** Craft & Interaction Designer; Level 3 may fit independent ownership. Do not default to Level 4 because the craft is strong.

## 2. Research-led practitioner
**Evidence:** Research plans, usability sessions, synthesis, and documented design changes; limited product metrics and technical governance.
**Expected:** Research-led Designer. Missing metrics are an evidence gap, not proof that research had no value.

## 3. Product-oriented designer
**Evidence:** Hypotheses, prioritization, experiments, and before/after user or product measures; limited reusable system work.
**Expected:** Product-oriented Designer. Positive metrics alone do not prove leadership or Level 4/5.

## 4. Systems concepts, adoption unknown
**Evidence:** Detailed design-system architecture, token taxonomy, governance proposals, and automation concepts; partial implementation and no adoption/outcome data.
**Expected:** Systems & DesignOps Designer may be provisional. Separate conceptual strength from execution/adoption/impact; do not automatically assign Level 4.

## 5. Operational DesignOps with adoption
**Evidence:** Implemented governance, multi-team use, documented contribution/release processes, repeatable quality or efficiency outcomes.
**Expected:** Systems & DesignOps Designer; Level 4 may fit if systemic execution is demonstrated. Level 5 requires sustained scalable change and durable impact.

## 6. Technical integration specialist
**Evidence:** Implemented design-to-code synchronization, APIs, component contracts, automated tests, and delivery workflows.
**Expected:** Technical Design Architect. Do not infer research or leadership capability from technical fluency.

## 7. AI prototype without reliability evidence
**Evidence:** AI prototypes exist; evaluation, failure handling, repeatability, human review, adoption, and impact are missing.
**Expected:** AI & Automation Innovator can be provisional/emerging. Do not treat prototypes as production-grade transformation.

## 8. Leadership and facilitation
**Evidence:** Documented workshops, decision records, mentoring, stakeholder alignment, conflict resolution, and cross-team outcomes.
**Expected:** Design Leader & Facilitator. Maturity depends on scope, consistency, execution, and impact.

## 9. Mixed evidence, no clear winner
**Evidence:** Moderate evidence across research, product, craft, and systems; no family clearly dominates and impact is incomplete.
**Expected:** Hybrid / Emerging profile; explain strongest combinations and what evidence would clarify the result.

## 10. Strategic vocabulary only
**Evidence:** Frequent strategy/systems/transformation language, but no artifacts, implementation, validation, adoption, or measured outcomes.
**Expected:** Do not classify as Strategic/Systemic or Transformational from vocabulary alone. Report insufficient evidence and low confidence.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Profile and maturity are separate outputs.
2. Different evidence scenarios do not all return the same strategic/systemic profile.
3. Proposals are not treated as implementation, adoption, or impact.
4. Hybrid / Emerging is allowed when no primary profile is clearly supported.
5. Uncertainty and counter-evidence are explicit.
6. Identical evidence yields materially consistent results; different evidence yields different profiles only when the evidence supports that difference.
